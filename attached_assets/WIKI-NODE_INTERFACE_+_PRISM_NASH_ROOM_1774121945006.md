# wiki/node interface area.


**The Room.** You want a single canvas workspace where an ingested reading arrives already annotated (summaries, highlights, curriculum-relevant tags, connections to your knowledge graph). Not raw text dumped in — processed text, with the annotations as first-class objects you can interact with.

**Reorderable linearity.** The reading has a sequence, but you want to be able to drag sections around to reorganize the logical flow for *your* purposes — maybe the conclusion goes first because that's what's relevant to Enlil's triangle, maybe you pull a middle section up because it connects to a Day A metaphor discussion. The reading's original order is preserved somewhere, but your working order is yours.

**Annotation worlds.** Each annotation is a portal. You highlight something and it creates a wiki-like node that connects to the tangent universe of that concept — other readings, other lessons, other nodes in your graph. You can *explore outward* from any annotation without leaving the room.

**Flowchart view.** A toggle or overlay that shows the reading as a flowchart — the structural logic of the argument or content, not just paragraphs. And the annotations you've made become draggable rounded boxes with adjustable sizes that you can place on this flowchart to build out the connections visually.

**The Prism.** This is the wildest and most interesting piece. A 3D sidebar — not a flat panel but a rotatable prism (or cube) where each *face* is a different category panel from your curriculum system (maybe one face is deity-element pairings, one is MAGIC drivers, one is lesson sections, one is research sources, one is standards). You rotate it like a Rubik's cube to bring different category sets into view, and each face has its own tabs and settings. So your tool panel isn't a flat sidebar with too many tabs — it's a spatial object you navigate.

**The flowchart construction mode.** Beyond just viewing a reading as a flowchart, you want to be able to *construct* knowledge connection maps — create new objects (nodes), define their types, draw connections between them, and specify the *nuance* of those connections (not just "A relates to B" but "A causes B," "A is the metaphoric register of B," "A contradicts B," etc.). Typed, directed edges with semantic meaning.

This is essentially your wiki-node-canvas-graphnode vision from January, but now with three major additions: the prism sidebar, the annotation-as-portal pattern, and the typed/directed connection builder.
