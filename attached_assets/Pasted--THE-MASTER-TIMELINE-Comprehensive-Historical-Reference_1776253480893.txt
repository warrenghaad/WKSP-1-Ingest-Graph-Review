# 🔥 PREHISTORIC ERA (300,000 BCE - 3,500 BCE)
### *The Dawn of Human Thinking*

## Mathematics (M)
- **Tally Marks (35,000 BCE):** First evidence of counting on bones and cave walls
  - *Grade Appropriateness:* K-2 (perfect for introducing one-to-one correspondence)
  - *Activity:* Make tally marks with sticks in mud or clay
- **Lunar Cycle Tracking (30,000 BCE):** Evidence of moon phase calendars
  - *Grade Appropriateness:* 2-5 (patterns, cycles, early astronomy)
  - *Activity:* Create a moon phase calendar over 30 days
- **Basic Patterns (20,000 BCE):** Repetition in art suggests pattern recognition
  - *Grade Appropriateness:* K-3 (pattern identification and creation)
- **Counting Systems:** Probably finger counting, body part counting
  - *Grade Appropriateness:* K-1 (embodied mathematics)

## Geometry/Art (G)
- **Cave Paintings (40,000 BCE - Chauvet, France):**
  - First known representational art
  - Use of charcoal and ochre pigments
  - Understanding of spatial composition
  - *Grade Appropriateness:* K-5 (all ages love cave art!)
  - *Activity:* Paint on brown paper with charcoal and natural pigments
- **Hand Stencils (37,000 BCE - Sulawesi, Indonesia):**
  - Negative space art technique
  - Evidence of symbolic thinking
  - *Activity:* Create hand stencils with spray bottles
- **Venus Figurines (30,000 BCE):**
  - Three-dimensional carving
  - Exaggerated proportions (fertility symbols)
  - *Grade Appropriateness:* 4-8 (understanding symbolism and form)
- **Rock Arrangements (Megalithic structures, 4,000 BCE):**
  - Stonehenge, Newgrange
  - Astronomical alignment
  - *Activity:* Build miniature stone circles, measure shadows

## Imagination/Myth (I)
- **Creation Myths:** Every culture has stories about how the world began
  - *Examples:* Sky gods, earth mothers, cosmic eggs, primordial waters
  - *Grade Appropriateness:* K-8 (age-appropriate versions)
- **Animal Spirit Stories:** Animals as guides, teachers, or ancestors
  - *Grade Appropriateness:* K-5
  - *Activity:* Create your own animal spirit guide story
- **Fire Myths:** How fire was stolen from gods or discovered
  - *Example:* Prometheus (later Greek), Aboriginal fire stories
  - *Grade Appropriateness:* 2-8
- **Star Stories:** Constellations as narrative maps
  - *Grade Appropriateness:* 2-8
  - *Activity:* Create constellation myths

## Inventions & Discoveries (Variables Combined)
1. **Controlled Fire (400,000 BCE+):**
   - M: Temperature control, fuel calculation
   - G: Light creating shadows, cave painting enabled
   - I: Fire myths across cultures
   - P: Control of fire = power in community
   - *Activity:* Safe fire observation, shadow art

2. **Language (200,000 BCE+):**
   - M: Symbolic representation
   - I: Storytelling becomes possible
   - P: Communication enables coordination
   - *SEL Connection:* Understanding how communication builds community

3. **Tools (2.6 million years ago+):**
   - M: Angles, leverage, proportion
   - G: Symmetrical hand axes show aesthetic sense
   - P: Better tools = more resources = power
   - *Activity:* Study simple machines (wedge, lever)

4. **Clothing & Needles (40,000 BCE):**
   - M: Measurement, patterns, counting stitches
   - G: Decoration, adornment, geometric patterns
   - P: Clothing signifies status
   - *Activity:* Simple weaving, stitching patterns

## Power Structures (P)
- **Tribal Organization:** Small groups, kinship-based
  - Decision-making: Elders, consensus
  - *SEL Connection:* Understanding group roles and cooperation
- **Shamans/Wisdom Keepers:** Spiritual and medical authority
  - Knowledge holders of myths, healing, astronomy
  - *SEL Connection:* Respecting different types of knowledge

## Famous Persons
*(While we don't have named individuals from this era, we honor the unnamed inventors and artists)*
- **The First Artist:** Unknown painter of Chauvet Cave
- **The First Mathematician:** Unknown person who first counted beyond "one, two, many"
- **The First Astronomer:** Unknown person who tracked moon cycles
- *Activity:* Imagine and write about "A Day in the Life of the First Artist"

# 🏛️ MESOPOTAMIA (3,500 BCE - 539 BCE)
### *The Cradle of Civilization - Where Writing and Cities Began*

## Mathematics (M)
- **Base-60 Number System (3,000 BCE):**
  - Why 60? Divisible by 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 10, 12, 15, 20, 30
  - *Still used today:* 60 seconds, 60 minutes, 360 degrees
  - *Grade Appropriateness:* 3-8 (understanding different bases)
  - *Activity:* Count to 60 using finger segments (12 per hand!)
  
- **Fractions:** First evidence of division and fractional thinking
  - *Grade Appropriateness:* 3-5
  - *Activity:* Divide clay "bread" into fair portions
  
- **Geometry of Angles:** Division of circle into 360 degrees
  - *Grade Appropriateness:* 4-8
  - *Activity:* Create a Mesopotamian protractor
  
- **Area Calculation:** For fields, buildings, city planning
  - *Grade Appropriateness:* 3-5
  - *Activity:* Calculate area of classroom "ziggurat"
  
- **Pythagorean Triple Knowledge (before Pythagoras!):**
  - Knew 3-4-5 triangle creates right angle
  - *Grade Appropriateness:* 5-8
  - *Activity:* Use rope with 12 knots to create right angles

## Geometry/Art (G)
- **Cuneiform Writing (3,200 BCE):**
  - Wedge-shaped marks made with stylus in clay
  - Evolution from pictographs to abstract symbols
  - Geometry of the wedge shape
  - *Grade Appropriateness:* 2-8 (all love clay writing!)
  - *Activity:* Create cuneiform tablets with clay and chopsticks
  
- **Cylinder Seals (3,400 BCE):**
  - Rolled on clay to create repeating patterns
  - Understanding of circumference and infinite repetition
  - *Grade Appropriateness:* K-5
  - *Activity:* Create own cylinder seal designs, roll on playdough
  
- **Ziggurats (2,100 BCE - Ur):**
  - Step pyramid temples
  - Understanding of structural load, geometry, and proportion
  - *Grade Appropriateness:* 3-8
  - *Activity:* Build ziggurat model with sugar cubes or blocks
  
- **Relief Carving:**
  - Bulls, lions, mythical creatures
  - Understanding of depth, perspective, proportion
  - *Grade Appropriateness:* 4-8
  - *Activity:* Create relief carving in clay or soap
  
- **Gate of Ishtar (575 BCE):**
  - Glazed brick mosaics
  - Tessellation patterns
  - Brilliant blue lapis lazuli color
  - *Activity:* Create paper mosaic of Ishtar Gate

## Imagination/Myth (I)
- **Epic of Gilgamesh (2,100 BCE):**
  - Oldest surviving great work of literature
  - Themes: Friendship, mortality, quest for immortality, flood story
  - *Grade Appropriateness:* 3-8 (age-appropriate versions)
  - *Activity:* Act out key scenes, discuss mortality themes
  
- **Tiamat Creation Myth:**
  - Primordial salt water goddess
  - Creation through conflict (Marduk defeats Tiamat)
  - Her body becomes the earth and sky
  - *Grade Appropriateness:* 3-8
  - *Activity:* Compare to other creation myths
  
- **Inanna's Descent to the Underworld:**
  - Journey through seven gates
  - Transformation and rebirth
  - *Grade Appropriateness:* 4-8
  - *Activity:* Draw the seven gates, discuss symbolic meaning
  
- **The Flood Story (in Gilgamesh):**
  - Utnapishtim survives great flood
  - Parallels to Noah, Deucalion, etc.
  - *Grade Appropriateness:* 3-8
  - *SEL Connection:* Why do cultures tell flood stories?

## Inventions & Discoveries (Variables Combined)
1. **The Wheel (3,500 BCE):**
   - M: Circle geometry, rotation, leverage
   - G: Perfect circle is aesthetically significant
   - I: Associated with sun gods (rolling sun across sky)
   - P: Transportation revolutionizes trade and warfare
   - *Activity:* Build a wheeled cart, calculate distance traveled
   
2. **Writing/Cuneiform (3,200 BCE):**
   - M: Record keeping enables complex calculations
   - G: Geometric shapes (wedges) become symbols
   - I: Stories can be preserved across generations
   - P: Literacy becomes source of power
   - *SEL Connection:* How does writing change communication?
   - *Activity:* Write a message in cuneiform, have friend decode
   
3. **Legal Code (Hammurabi's Code, 1,754 BCE):**
   - M: Quantified punishments (mathematical justice)
   - G: Carved on stone stele (visual representation)
   - I: Justice as divine mandate
   - P: Written law = centralized authority
   - *Activity:* Discuss fairness: "Eye for an eye" vs. other systems
   - *SEL Connection:* Justice, fairness, consequences
   
4. **Beer Brewing (3,000 BCE):**
   - M: Fermentation timing, ratio of ingredients
   - G: Container design for brewing
   - I: Sacred drink, offered to gods
   - P: Payment for labor (workers paid in beer!)
   - *Activity:* Study fermentation with yeast and sugar (visual observation)
   
5. **Irrigation Systems:**
   - M: Water flow calculations, volume
   - G: Canal design, aqueduct architecture
   - I: Water as life-giving divine gift
   - P: Control of water = control of agriculture = power
   - *Activity:* Build model irrigation system with clay channels

## Power Structures (P)
- **City-States:** Independent cities (Ur, Uruk, Babylon) with surrounding farmland
  - Competition and alliances between cities
  - *SEL Connection:* Cooperation vs. competition
  
- **Kingship:** Divine or divinely-appointed rulers
  - Priest-kings combining religious and political authority
  - *SEL Connection:* Different types of leadership
  
- **Social Hierarchy:**
  - Priests/Kings → Nobles → Merchants → Artisans → Farmers → Slaves
  - Rigid class system
  - *SEL Connection:* Justice, inequality, privilege
  
- **Scribal Schools:**
  - Elite education for boys
  - Literacy = access to power
  - *Activity:* Discuss who had access to education—why does it matter?

## Famous Persons

### **Enheduanna (2,285 BCE - 2,250 BCE)**
- **Who:** High priestess of the moon god, daughter of Sargon of Akkad
- **Achievement:** First known author in history (by name)
- **What She Created:** Hymns and poems to goddess Inanna
- **Why Important:** Proves women were authors and thinkers in ancient world
- **Variable Focus:** Imagination/Myth (storytelling) + Power (religious authority)
- **Grade Appropriateness:** 3-8
- **SEL Connection:** Challenging assumptions about who creates knowledge
- **Activity:** Read her poetry, write hymn to something you honor

### **Hammurabi (1,810 BCE - 1,750 BCE)**
- **Who:** King of Babylon
- **Achievement:** Created famous law code (282 laws carved in stone)
- **Why Important:** One of earliest written legal systems
- **Variable Focus:** Power (governance) + Mathematics (quantified justice)
- **Grade Appropriateness:** 4-8
- **Example Law:** "If a builder builds a house and it collapses and kills the owner, the builder shall be put to death."
- **SEL Connection:** Justice, consequences, fairness
- **Activity:** Write your own class code of conduct, discuss fairness

### **Sin-liqe-unninni (around 1,300 BCE)**
- **Who:** Scribe who compiled the standard version of the Epic of Gilgamesh
- **Achievement:** Preserved and organized the world's oldest epic
- **Variable Focus:** Imagination/Myth (storytelling preservation)
- **Grade Appropriateness:** 4-8
- **Activity:** Research what makes a story "epic," create your own epic outline

### **Anonymous Inventor of the Wheel**
- **Achievement:** Changed human civilization forever
- **Variable Focus:** All four—Mathematics (geometry), Art (circular form), Imagination (revolutionary thinking), Power (transportation revolution)
- **Grade Appropriateness:** K-8
- **Activity:** Imagine life before the wheel—how would you transport things?

# 🐫 EGYPT (3,100 BCE - 30 BCE)
### *Land of Pyramids, Pharaohs, and Fractions*

## Mathematics (M)
- **Unit Fractions (2,000 BCE - Rhind Mathematical Papyrus):**
  - Expressed all fractions as sum of unit fractions: 2/5 = 1/3 + 1/15
  - Why? Philosophical preference for dividing whole into parts
  - *Grade Appropriateness:* 4-8
  - *Activity:* Solve problems using only unit fractions
  
- **Geometry (3-4-5 Right Triangle):**
  - Used "rope stretchers" to create right angles for building
  - 3-4-5 triangle perfect for surveying
  - *Grade Appropriateness:* 4-8
  - *Activity:* Become a rope stretcher—create right angles with rope
  
- **Volume Calculations:**
  - Calculated volume of granaries (cylinders and rectangular prisms)
  - *Formula (approximate):* V = (8/9 × d)² × h for cylinder
  - *Grade Appropriateness:* 5-8
  - *Activity:* Calculate volume of your own "granary" containers
  
- **Surveying After Nile Floods:**
  - Land boundaries washed away annually
  - Needed to re-measure fields every year
  - Birth of geometry (geo = earth, metry = measurement)
  - *Grade Appropriateness:* 3-8
  - *Activity:* "Flood" a sandbox or tray, re-measure boundaries
  
- **Area of Triangle:**
  - Formula: A = 1/2 × base × height (they knew this!)
  - *Grade Appropriateness:* 4-8
  
- **Pi Approximation:**
  - Estimated π ≈ 3.16 (close to 3.14159...)
  - Used for circular calculations
  - *Grade Appropriateness:* 5-8
  - *Activity:* Measure circumferences and diameters to rediscover pi

## Geometry/Art (G)
- **Hieroglyphics (3,200 BCE):**
  - Sacred writing combining logographic and alphabetic elements
  - Beautiful, precise geometric shapes
  - *Grade Appropriateness:* K-8 (all ages love hieroglyphics!)
  - *Activity:* Write name in hieroglyphics using a cartouche
  
- **Pyramids (2,580 BCE - Great Pyramid of Giza):**
  - **Mathematical Achievement:**
    - Precise right angles and orientation to cardinal directions
    - Height:base ratio approximates golden ratio
    - Original height: 146.5 meters (481 feet)
    - Base: 230.4 meters each side (nearly perfect square!)
    - Built with 2.3 million stone blocks
  - **Geometric Beauty:** Perfect pyramid shape, alignment with Orion's belt
  - *Grade Appropriateness:* 3-8 (K-2 can do simpler versions)
  - *Activity:* Build scale pyramid, calculate ratio of blocks needed
  
- **Profile Perspective (Egyptian Artistic Canon):**
  - Head in profile, eye from front
  - Torso from front, legs in profile  
  - Not "wrong"—a deliberate artistic choice showing all important features
  - Hierarchical scale (pharaoh larger than others)
  - *Grade Appropriateness:* 2-8
  - *Activity:* Draw yourself in Egyptian profile style
  
- **Papyrus:**
  - First practical paper-like writing surface
  - Geometric process of layering and pressing reed strips
  - *Activity:* Make "papyrus" from paper strips and glue
  
- **Cartouches:**
  - Oval name rings for royalty
  - Geometric perfection of the oval
  - *Grade Appropriateness:* K-8
  - *Activity:* Create decorated cartouche with your name
  
- **Obelisks:**
  - Tall, four-sided, narrow pyramidions at top
  - Understanding of center of gravity and balance
  - Often carved from single piece of granite
  - *Activity:* Design and balance own obelisk structure

## Imagination/Myth (I)
- **Isis and Osiris:**
  - Osiris (god of afterlife) murdered by brother Set
  - Isis (goddess of magic) resurrects him
  - Themes: Death, resurrection, love, justice
  - *Grade Appropriateness:* 3-8
  - *Activity:* Act out story, discuss themes of justice and loyalty
  
- **Ra's Journey:**
  - Sun god Ra travels across sky by day in solar barque
  - Battles chaos serpent Apophis each night
  - Daily cycle = eternal struggle of order vs. chaos
  - *Grade Appropriateness:* 2-8
  - *Activity:* Create sun boat artwork, discuss daily cycles
  
- **Weighing of the Heart (Ma'at):**
  - After death, heart weighed against feather of Ma'at (truth/justice)
  - If heavy with sin, devoured by Ammit
  - If balanced, proceed to afterlife
  - *Grade Appropriateness:* 3-8
  - *SEL Connection:* Conscience, ethics, life choices
  - *Activity:* Create scales of Ma'at, discuss fairness
  
- **Pharaoh Divinity:**
  - Pharaoh believed to be living god (Horus)
  - Divine right to rule
  - Responsible for Ma'at (order, balance, truth)
  - *Activity:* Discuss different ideas about leadership and authority

## Inventions & Discoveries (Variables Combined)
1. **Pyramids (2,580 BCE - Construction Methods):**
   - M: Precise calculations, geometry, volume, labor management
   - G: Perfect form, golden ratio proportions, architectural achievement
   - I: Stairway to heaven, eternal monument, divine connection
   - P: Monument to pharaoh's power, required massive coordination
   - *Activity:* Research and debate: How were they built? (ramps, levers, sledges)
   
2. **Papyrus Making (3,000 BCE):**
   - M: Precision in layering and sizing
   - G: Beautiful surface for writing and art
   - I: Preserved stories and knowledge
   - P: Enabled bureaucracy, record-keeping
   - *Activity:* Make "papyrus" and write hieroglyphic message
   
3. **Mummification:**
   - M: Precise timing (70-day process), careful measurement of materials
   - G: Aesthetic wrapping patterns, beautiful sarcophagi
   - I: Preservation for afterlife, ritual significance
   - P: Expensive process = marker of status
   - *Activity:* "Mummify" an apple (baking soda/salt preservation)
   - *SEL Connection:* Different cultures' beliefs about death
   
4. **Calendar (365 days):**
   - M: Mathematical tracking of solar year
   - G: Visual representation of cycles
   - I: Alignment with agricultural and religious festivals
   - P: Control of time = political/religious power
   - *Activity:* Create Egyptian calendar with flood season, planting, harvest
   
5. **Obelisk Raising:**
   - M: Leverage, center of gravity calculations
   - G: Monumental sculpture, perfect geometry
   - I: Rays of sun god Ra in stone
   - P: Display of engineering prowess and resources
   - *Activity:* Physics of levers—raise a "heavy" object with fulcrum

## Power Structures (P)
- **Divine Kingship (Pharaoh):**
  - Pharaoh = god on earth (Horus)
  - Absolute authority, intermediary between gods and humans
  - *SEL Connection:* Different forms of leadership
  
- **Social Hierarchy:**
  - Pharaoh → Vizier → Priests → Nobles → Scribes → Craftsmen → Farmers → Slaves
  - Rigid but some mobility (scribes could rise)
  - *Activity:* Discuss: What would it be like to live in each class?
  
- **Temples & Priesthood:**
  - Enormous economic and political power
  - Controlled vast lands and resources
  - Performed rituals to maintain Ma'at (cosmic order)
  
- **Scribes:**
  - Literacy = power and privilege
  - "Be a scribe! Save yourself from hard labor!"
  - *Activity:* Practice hieroglyphics—feel the precision required

## Famous Persons

### **Imhotep (2650-2600 BCE)**
- **Who:** Architect, physician, high priest, and polymath
- **Achievement:** Designed the Step Pyramid at Saqqara (first pyramid!)
- **Variable Focus:** Mathematics + Geometry (architectural genius), Power (rose from commoner to legend)
- **Why Important:** Later deified as god of medicine and wisdom
- **Grade Appropriateness:** 4-8
- **SEL Connection:** Growth mindset—rose through merit and skill
- **Activity:** Design your own step pyramid, calculate dimensions

### **Hatshepsut (1507-1458 BCE)**
- **Who:** Female pharaoh who ruled as king (not queen regent)
- **Achievement:** Peaceful reign, impressive architecture (Temple at Deir el-Bahari)
- **Variable Focus:** Power (challenged gender norms), Geometry (architectural patron)
- **Why Important:** Most successful female pharaoh, wore false beard and ruled in her own right
- **Grade Appropriateness:** 3-8
- **SEL Connection:* Challenging gender stereotypes, leadership courage
- **Activity:** Research and present on Hatshepsut's trade expedition to Punt

### **Ahmes (1680-1620 BCE)**
- **Who:** Scribe who copied the Rhind Mathematical Papyrus
- **Achievement:** Preserved mathematical knowledge (85 problems with solutions)
- **Variable Focus:** Mathematics (unit fractions, geometry, practical problems)
- **Why Important:** Our main source for understanding Egyptian mathematics
- **Grade Appropriateness:** 5-8
- **Activity:** Solve problems from the Rhind Papyrus (adapted for grade level)

### **Nefertiti (1370-1330 BCE)**
- **Who:** Queen of Egypt, wife of Akhenaten
- **Achievement:** Religious revolution (monotheism of Aten), iconic beauty
- **Variable Focus:** Imagination/Myth (religious reform), Power (co-ruler), Art (famous bust)
- **Grade Appropriateness:** 4-8
- **Fun Fact:** Her famous bust is one of most recognizable artworks from ancient world
- **Activity:** Study and draw or sculpt the Nefertiti bust, discuss idealized beauty

### **Tutankhamun (1341-1323 BCE)**
- **Who:** "King Tut"—boy pharaoh who died young
- **Achievement:** Not particularly notable in life, but his intact tomb revolutionized archaeology
- **Why Important:** Howard Carter's 1922 discovery revealed incredible artifacts
- **Variable Focus:** All four—tomb shows math, art, myth, and power of ancient Egypt
- **Grade Appropriateness:** K-8 (all ages love King Tut!)
- **Activity:** Catalog objects from tomb (like an archaeologist), discuss preservation

# 🏺 GREECE (800 BCE - 146 BCE)
### *The Birth of Proof, Philosophy, and Democracy*

## Mathematics (M)
- **Proof-Based Mathematics (600-300 BCE):**
  - Revolutionary idea: Math isn't just practical—it's about logical certainty
  - "Why is it true?" not just "Does it work?"
  - *Grade Appropriateness:* 5-8 (introducing logical reasoning)
  
- **Pythagoras (570-495 BCE):**
  - Pythagorean Theorem: a² + b² = c²
  - "All is number"—mathematical mysticism
  - Discovered irrational numbers (√2), which disturbed them!
  - *Grade Appropriateness:* 6-8 (algebraic concepts)
  - *Activity:* Prove Pythagorean theorem with paper squares
  
- **Euclid (300 BCE - "Elements"):**
  - Most influential mathematics textbook ever written
  - Axiomatic method: Start with basic assumptions, build logically
  - 13 books covering geometry, number theory, spatial reasoning
  - *Grade Appropriateness:* 6-8 (introduction to proofs)
  - *Activity:* Work through simple propositions from Book I
  
- **Golden Ratio (Φ ≈ 1.618):**
  - Found in Parthenon proportions, nautilus shells, spiral galaxies
  - *Grade Appropriateness:* 5-8
  - *Activity:* Find golden ratio in nature and architecture
  
- **Archimedes (287-212 BCE):**
  - Calculated pi accurately
  - Discovered principle of buoyancy ("Eureka!")
  - Method of exhaustion (precursor to calculus)
  - Invented death-ray mirrors (allegedly!)
  - *Grade Appropriateness:* 5-8
  - *Activity:* Test buoyancy with clay boats
  
- **Infinity and Paradoxes (Zeno):**
  - Achilles and the Tortoise, Arrow Paradox
  - Introduced concept of infinite series
  - *Grade Appropriateness:* 7-8 (challenging logical thinking)

## Geometry/Art (G)
- **Parthenon (447-432 BCE):**
  - Golden ratio proportions throughout
  - Optical illusions: Columns bulge slightly, floor curves up slightly
  - Why? To appear perfectly straight! (compensating for visual distortion)
  - *Grade Appropriateness:* 4-8
  - *Activity:* Study optical illusions in architecture
  
- **Greek Pottery:**
  - Black-figure and red-figure techniques
  - Narrative art telling myths
  - Understanding of rotation and cylindrical surfaces
  - *Grade Appropriateness:* 3-8
  - *Activity:* Design own Greek pottery with mythological scene
  
- **Sculpture:**
  - Idealized human form
  - Contrapposto (weight shift creating dynamic pose)
  - Mathematical proportions for "perfect" body
  - *Grade Appropriateness:* 5-8
  - *Activity:* Study proportions, draw figure in contrapposto
  
- **Theater Architecture:**
  - Semicircular amphitheaters with perfect acoustics
  - Geometry of sound reflection
  - *Grade Appropriateness:* 5-8
  - *Activity:* Test acoustics in different shaped rooms

## Imagination/Myth (I)
- **Olympic Gods:**
  - Zeus, Hera, Athena, Apollo, Artemis, Aphrodite, Ares, Hermes, Hephaestus, Poseidon, Demeter, Dionysus
  - Human-like gods with flaws, passions, and conflicts
  - *Grade Appropriateness:* 2-8 (age-appropriate versions)
  
- **Hero Myths:**
  - **Hercules/Heracles:** 12 labors (strength, redemption)
  - **Perseus:** Slaying Medusa (courage, cleverness)
  - **Theseus:** Minotaur and the labyrinth (intelligence, courage)
  - **Odysseus:** 10-year journey home (cunning, perseverance)
  - **Jason:** Golden Fleece quest (leadership, adventure)
  - *Grade Appropriateness:* 3-8
  - *Activity:* Map a hero's journey, identify trials and growth
  
- **Trojan War:**
  - Helen of Troy, Achilles, Hector, wooden horse
  - Themes: Honor, pride, fate, cleverness
  - *Grade Appropriateness:* 4-8
  
- **Prometheus:**
  - Stole fire from gods to give to humans
  - Punishment: Chained, eagle eats liver daily (regenerates nightly)
  - Theme: Knowledge comes with consequences
  - *Grade Appropriateness:* 4-8
  - *SEL Connection:* Discussing sacrifice and consequences of defying authority

## Inventions & Discoveries (Variables Combined)
1. **Democracy (507 BCE - Athens):**
   - M: Vote counting, majority rule calculations
   - G: Assembly spaces (agora), public architecture
   - I: Ideal of citizen participation
   - P: Revolutionary power-sharing (though limited: only adult male citizens)
   - *Activity:* Hold classroom democracy—debate and vote on issue
   - *SEL Connection:* Civic responsibility, voice, participation
   
2. **The Proof (Mathematical Reasoning):**
   - M: Logic, deductive reasoning, QED
   - I: Pursuit of truth and certainty
   - P: Knowledge as power, intellectual hierarchy
   - *Activity:* Simple geometric proof with visual aids
   
3. **Aqueducts:**
   - M: Gradient calculations (water flows downhill)
   - G: Arched architecture, engineering beauty
   - I: Bringing life-giving water from mountains
   - P: Public works, civic engineering
   - *Activity:* Build aqueduct with paper and test water flow
   
4. **Astrolabe:**
   - M: Celestial calculations, star positions
   - G: Circular instrument design
   - I: Connecting earth to cosmos
   - P: Navigation = exploration and trade power
   - *Activity:* Create simple astrolabe, observe star positions
   
5. **Theater & Drama:**
   - M: Acoustic geometry
   - G: Amphitheater architecture
   - I: Storytelling, catharsis, tragedy, comedy
   - P: Civic ritual, shared cultural experience
   - *Activity:* Perform short scene from Greek play

## Power Structures (P)
- **Democracy in Athens:**
  - Direct democracy for citizens
  - Assembly (Ecclesia), Council (Boule), Courts
  - **BUT:** Only adult male citizens (excluded women, slaves, foreigners)
  - *SEL Connection:* Who is included/excluded? What is justice?
  
- **Sparta (Contrast):**
  - Military oligarchy
  - Dual kings, council of elders
  - Focus on military training from youth
  - *Activity:* Compare Athenian vs. Spartan education
  
- **Slavery:**
  - Foundation of Greek economy
  - Enslaved people did much labor, enabling citizens to philosophize
  - *SEL Connection:* Discussing uncomfortable truths, historical inequality
  
- **Gender Roles:**
  - Women largely excluded from public life (except religious roles)
  - Notable exception: Spartan women had more freedom
  - *Activity:* Research Sappho (female poet), discuss historical gender inequality

## Famous Persons

### **Pythagoras (570-495 BCE)**
- **Who:** Mathematician, philosopher, cult leader
- **Achievement:** Pythagorean theorem, mathematical mysticism
- **Variable Focus:** Mathematics (proofs, ratios, geometry)
- **Strange Fact:** His followers weren't allowed to eat beans!
- **Grade Appropriateness:* 5-8
- **Activity:** Prove Pythagorean theorem with visual/manipulative method

### **Socrates (470-399 BCE)**
- **Who:** Philosopher who asked endless questions
- **Achievement:** Socratic method (learning through questioning)
- **Quote:** "The unexamined life is not worth living"
- **Variable Focus:** Imagination (new ways of thinking), Power (challenged authority, sentenced to death)
- **Grade Appropriateness:** 6-8
- **SEL Connection:** Critical thinking, questioning assumptions
- **Activity:** Socratic seminar on ethical question

### **Plato (428-348 BCE)**
- **Who:** Student of Socrates, founded Academy
- **Achievement:** Theory of Forms, "Allegory of the Cave"
- **Variable Focus:** Imagination (ideal forms), Mathematics (geometry as path to truth)
- **Famous Quote:** "Let no one ignorant of geometry enter here" (at Academy entrance)
- **Grade Appropriateness:** 7-8
- **Activity:** Read simplified "Allegory of Cave," discuss what is "real"

### **Aristotle (384-322 BCE)**
- **Who:** Student of Plato, tutor to Alexander the Great
- **Achievement:** Founded formal logic, categorization of knowledge
- **Variable Focus:** All four—studied everything!
- **Grade Appropriateness:** 7-8
- **Activity:** Practice categorical logic (syllogisms)

### **Euclid (300 BCE)**
- **Who:** "Father of Geometry"
- **Achievement:** "Elements"—most influential textbook ever
- **Variable Focus:** Mathematics + Geometry (axiomatic proofs)
- **Grade Appropriateness:** 6-8
- **Activity:** Work through simple propositions from Elements Book I

### **Archimedes (287-212 BCE)**
- **Who:** Greatest mathematician of antiquity
- **Achievement:** Pi calculation, buoyancy principle, war machines
- **Famous Story:** Ran naked through street shouting "Eureka!" after discovering displacement principle in bath
- **Variable Focus:** Mathematics (calculations) + Engineering (machines)
- **Grade Appropriateness:** 5-8
- **Activity:** Test Archimedes' principle with different objects in water

### **Hypatia (350-415 CE)** *(Late Ancient Greece/Early Byzantine)*
- **Who:** Female mathematician, astronomer, philosopher in Alexandria
- **Achievement:** Preserved and taught Greek mathematical works
- **Why Important:** Rare documented female scholar; martyred by religious mob
- **Variable Focus:** Mathematics + Power (challenged by rising Christianity)
- **Grade Appropriateness:** 6-8
- **SEL Connection:** Gender in STEM, religious conflict, loss of knowledge
- **Activity:** Research Hypatia's life, discuss preservation of knowledge
