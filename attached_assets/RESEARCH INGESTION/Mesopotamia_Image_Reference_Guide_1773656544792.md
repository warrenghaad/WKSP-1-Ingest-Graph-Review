### IMAGE 1: Akkadian Hunting Cylinder Seal
**DIRECT URL**: https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/329090
**Object ID**: 329090
**Period**: Akkadian (2350-2150 BCE)
**Material**: Stone cylinder with modern impression shown
**Dimensions**: ~2-3 cm height typical

**WHAT STUDENTS SEE**:
- Hunter grasping ibex between two trees
- Cuneiform inscription reading "Balu-ili, cupbearer"
- Ibexes on mountains flanking three trees

- Both seal and "rolled out" impression visible

**PEDAGOGICAL APPLICATIONS**:

**Grades K-2**:

- *Math*: Identify symmetry (trees flanking animals)
- *Literacy*: "Read" the story left-to-right like early narrative art
- *Social Studies*: "Balu-ili was a cupbearer (royal servant). His JOB was on his seal!"
- *STEM*: "How does a 3D cylinder make a 2D picture?"

**Grades 6-8**:
- *Advanced Math*: Calculate cylinder circumference to image length ratio
- *History*: Professional identity + personal ownership in ancient bureaucracy
- *Critical Thinking*: "Why would ancient people need 'signatures'? What does this tell us about their society?"

**WHAT STUDENTS SEE**:
- Large brick with cuneiform stamp
- Clear wedge-shaped impressions
- **DOG PAW PRINTS** pressed accidentally into wet clay!
- King Ur-Nammu's name and titles

**PEDAGOGICAL APPLICATIONS**:

**Grades K-2**:
- *Observation*: "Find the dog paw prints! A dog walked across this brick 4,000 years ago!"
- *Process Learning*: "The clay was WET (like playdough), then they baked it hard"
- *Wedge Shapes*: "All the writing is made of just a few shapes - like Lego bricks!"

**Grades 3-5**:
- *Social Studies*: "King Ur-Nammu built the Great Ziggurat. This brick is FROM that building!"

- *Math*: Brick dimensions, standardization, calculate bricks needed for ziggurat (720,000)

**Grades 6-8**:
- *Engineering*: Brick standardization = modular construction (like LEGO system!)
- *Archaeology*: Accidental evidence (dog prints) = daily life details

- **Historical Thinking*: "One brick from a MASSIVE structure - how does one piece help us understand the whole?"

2. Add "accidental" marks (thumb print, pencil poke)
3. Let dry
4. Discuss: "What might archaeologists learn from YOUR brick 4,000 years from now?"

### IMAGE 7: Cuneiform Mathematical Tablet - YBC 7290  
**ACCESS**: CDLI (Cuneiform Digital Library Initiative) - cdli.ucla.edu
**Search**: "YBC 7290" or "Yale Babylonian Collection 7290"
**Object**: Clay tablet showing square with diagonals
**Content**: Demonstrates √2 ≈ 1.414213 (accurate to 6 decimal places!)
**Period**: 1800-1600 BCE (Old Babylonian)
**Dimensions**: 72 × 78 × 27 mm

**WHAT STUDENTS SEE**:
- Square with two diagonal lines
- Numbers along sides in cuneiform
- Calculation of diagonal length using square root

**PEDAGOGICAL APPLICATIONS**:

**Grades 3-5**:
- *Geometry*: "Draw a square, draw both diagonals - it makes triangles!"
- *Measurement*: "If each side is 1 unit, how long is the diagonal?"
- *Intro to Roots*: "It's a special number - not quite 1, not quite 1.5..."

**Grades 6-8**:
- *Pythagorean Theorem*: a² + b² = c², so 1² + 1² = c², c = √2
- *Irrational Numbers*: "This number NEVER ENDS and NEVER REPEATS"
- *History of Math*: "Babylonians knew this 1,400 years before Pythagoras!"
- *Number Systems*: Show √2 in base-60 vs. base-10
- *Approximation*: "How did they calculate this without calculators?"

**STANDARDS ALIGNMENT**:
- CCSS.MATH.CONTENT.8.EE.A.2 (Square and cube roots)
- CCSS.MATH.CONTENT.8.G.B.7 (Pythagorean Theorem)

**MIND-BLOWING FACT FOR STUDENTS**:
"This clay tablet shows math so advanced that European mathematicians didn't understand it until 1,000 years ago. The Babylonians were mathematical GENIUSES."

### IMAGE 8: Plimpton 322 - Pythagorean Triple Table
**ACCESS**: Columbia University Rare Book Library / CDLI / Educational sources
**Search**: "Plimpton 322 tablet"
**Content**: 15 rows of Pythagorean triples (3-4-5, 5-12-13, etc.)
**Period**: ~1800 BCE (Old Babylonian)
**Dimensions**: 13 × 9 × 2 cm
**License**: Educational use with attribution (check institution)

**WHAT STUDENTS SEE**:
- Clay tablet with 4 columns of numbers in cuneiform
- Organized table format
- Right edge broken off (original had 5-6 columns)

**PEDAGOGICAL APPLICATIONS**:

**Grades 6-8** (Advanced):
- *Pythagorean Theorem*: "These are all right triangles: 3²+4²=5², etc."
- *Pattern Recognition*: "Can you find the pattern in how the numbers increase?"
- *Number Theory*: Integer solutions to a²+b²=c²
- *Historical Significance*: "This is 1,300 years BEFORE the Greek mathematician Pythagoras!"
- *Debate*: "Did Babylonians understand the theorem, or just know the triples?"

**RESEARCH EXTENSION**:
- Students investigate: "How might ancient builders use these triples?"
- Answer: Creating perfect right angles for buildings without modern tools
- Method: Rope with 12 evenly-spaced knots → 3-4-5 triangle

**WHY THIS MATTERS**:
"For over 100 years, historians thought Greeks invented advanced geometry. Then in 1945, a scholar realized what this tablet REALLY was - and it changed history."

**WHAT STUDENTS SEE**:
- Massive mud-brick pyramid with three terraces
- Monumental staircase ascending center
- Reconstructed sections vs. eroded sections visible
- Desert landscape for scale
- Human figures at base (tiny!) showing enormous size

**PEDAGOGICAL APPLICATIONS**:

**Grades K-2**:
- *3D Shapes*: "It's a stepped pyramid - like stairs for giants!"
- *Size Comparison*: "See the tiny people at the bottom? The ziggurat is HUGE!"
- *Pattern*: "Big step, medium step, small step on top"
- *Purpose*: "This was a temple - a house for a god"

**Grades 3-5**:
- *Geometry*: Rectangular pyramid, terraced levels, symmetrical staircases
- *Measurement*: Base 64m × 46m, height ~30m
- *Volume**: "How many bricks?" (Answer: 720,000!)
- *Architecture*: "Why build UP instead of OUT?" (Answer: Closer to heaven/sky gods)
- *Engineering*: Weight distribution, drainage systems

**Grades 6-8**:
- *Mathematical Modeling*: Calculate volume of each terrace
- *Surface Area*: Total brick coverage needed
- *Proportional Reasoning*: Each terrace smaller by specific ratio
- *Physics*: Center of gravity, structural stability
- *Cultural Significance*: Mountain symbolism (gods dwell on mountains, but Mesopotamia is FLAT - solution: build artificial mountain!)

**ACTIVITY IDEAS**:

**K-2**: Build ziggurat from blocks/boxes (big bottom, medium middle, small top)

**3-5**: "Brick Calculator" - If each brick weighs 15 kg, how many tons is the whole ziggurat?

**6-8**: "Engineering Challenge" - Design a stable terraced structure using specific base-to-height ratios

**CROSS-CURRICULAR CONNECTION**:
- **Social Studies**: Ziggurats = religious + political + economic centers (all-in-one!)
- **Science**: Mud-brick composition, baking process, weathering over 4,000 years
- **Art**: Compare to Egyptian pyramids (smooth vs. stepped), Mesoamerican pyramids (stepped with temples on top)

## SECTION 7: CLASSROOM IMPLEMENTATION GUIDE

### LOW-TECH APPROACH
**Materials Needed**:
- Printer (color if possible)
- Paper/cardstock
- Laminator (optional, for durability)

**Process**:
1. Download high-res images from URLs provided
2. Print at various sizes:
   - 8.5"×11" for student handouts
   - 11"×17" for small group work
   - Poster size for classroom display
3. Create "Artifact Cards" with:
   - Front: Image
   - Back: Key facts, discussion questions

### HIGH-TECH APPROACH
**Tools**:
- Interactive whiteboard
- Digital projector
- Tablets/Chromebooks

**Process**:
1. Create Google Slides presentation with embedded images
2. Use zoom function to examine details
3. Students annotate images digitally
4. Share collaborative findings via Google Classroom

### HANDS-ON EXTENSIONS

**CYLINDER SEAL WORKSHOP**:
- Materials: Model Magic/Playdough, cardboard tubes, toothpicks
- Process:
  1. Roll tube in clay/dough
  2. Carve design on tube surface
  3. Roll again to see impression
  4. Compare to ancient seals

**CUNEIFORM WRITING**:
- Materials: Air-dry clay, chopsticks/popsicle sticks
- Process:
  1. Flatten clay into tablet
  2. Press stick at angle = vertical wedge
  3. Press stick horizontal = horizontal wedge
  4. Write numbers 1-59 in base-60
  5. Let dry, display

**ZIGGURAT CONSTRUCTION**:
- Materials: Cardboard boxes (graduated sizes), paint
- Process:
  1. Stack boxes largest-to-smallest
  2. Paint mud-brick texture
  3. Add staircases (craft sticks)
  4. Calculate proportions
  5. Display with measurement labels

## SECTION 9: CULTURAL SENSITIVITY & CONTEXT

### IRAQ TODAY
**Important Context for Students**:
- "These ancient cities are in modern Iraq"
- "Iraq has experienced war and conflict - many ancient sites have been damaged"
- "Archaeologists and Iraqi people work to protect these treasures for the world"

### MUSEUM ETHICS
**Discussion Point** (Grades 6-8):
- "Many Mesopotamian artifacts are in Western museums (British Museum, Met, Louvre)"
- "Some were excavated 100+ years ago when rules were different"
- "Today, Iraq and other countries ask for artifacts to return home"
- "What do you think is fair?"

### CULTURAL APPRECIATION vs. APPROPRIATION
- **Respect**: Study, learn, appreciate
- **Give Credit**: "This is from ancient Mesopotamia (modern Iraq)"
- **Context**: Don't use sacred symbols without understanding
- **Example**: Cylinder seals were personal identity markers - creating craft versions for learning is respectful, using actual ancient religious symbols decoratively is not
