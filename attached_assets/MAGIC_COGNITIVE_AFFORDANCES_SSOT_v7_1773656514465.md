# MAGIC Cognitive Affordances — Section-Level Objectives
## SSOT: Translating Weight Vectors into Stackable Lesson Architecture

**Version:** 1.0 (March 2026)
**Canonical Source:** LESSON_ARCHITECTURE_SSOT_v7.md (15 sections: A1–A7 + B1–B8)
**Status:** Canonical reference for lesson production, e-textbook assembly, teacher frontend, and student demonstration specs
**Principle:** The MAGIC weight vector for each section IS the cognitive affordance specification. The loud actors' questions define the cognitive operation. Content changes per week; operations do not.

## How This Document Works

Each section below contains:

1. **Cognitive Operation** — the v7 canonical operation name
2. **MAGIC Vector** — the weight profile from v7 architecture
3. **Loud Actors** — which MAGIC questions drive the section
4. **Cognitive Affordance** — the verbal translation of the vector into a transferable cognitive operation
5. **Student Demonstration** — how students show they performed the operation (assessment anchor)
6. **Artifact Relevance** — what kind of source material this section requires from the RWI pipeline
7. **E-Textbook Role** — what this section contributes to the student-facing publication (Level 5)
8. **Teacher Deliverable** — what the teacher needs to execute this section (Levels 1 + 4)

### Stacking Logic (Within a Week)

Sections stack vertically through two arcs:

**Day A — The Rhetoric Arc:**
- **A1 → A2**: PRESENT establishes significance; DEFINE names the GEpHR mechanism (three-stage broadcast: Significance → Meaning → Elaboration)
- **A2 → A3**: The visual rhetoric principle from A2 is GENERALIZED across sacred art
- **A3 → A4**: Sacred generalization expands to social/secular diffusion — power (C) enters
- **A4 → A5**: Broad diffusion narrows to ONE artifact where A2's principle is materially CRYSTALLIZED
- **A5 → A6**: Crystallization → student PRACTICE of the GEA→GEM construction sequence
- **A6 → A7**: Making reveals the PIVOT — same geometry MEANS and DOES

**Day B — The Function Arc:**
- **A7 → B1**: The circumnutating bridge — same artifact, register shifts
- **B1 → B2**: Bridge PRESENTS functional frame; B2 DEFINES the mathematical property (= A2 in function register)
- **B2 → B3**: Mathematical property is GENERALIZED through GEK-T operations
- **B3 → B4**: Operations generalize through historical LINEAGE — G(t) trajectory, power re-enters
- **B4 → B5**: Lineage narrows to ONE invention — the STEM notch CRYSTALLIZATION (= A5 in function register)
- **B5 → B6**: Invention crystallized → TEACH the engineering decomposition for building
- **B6 → B7**: Decomposition taught → students PRACTICE building and testing
- **B7 → B8**: Build practice → SYNTHESIZE both registers + CIRCUIT to next week

### Nesting Logic (Across Weeks)

The SAME cognitive affordance deepens week over week:

- Week 1 A2 and Week 5 A2 ask the same question ("What perceptual affordance does this GEA/GEM composition produce?") but the student's capacity to answer has compounded through four prior cycles
- B8 of Week N feeds into A1 of Week N+1 — each week's synthesis becomes the next week's prior knowledge
- By Week 8, the MAGIC questions are automatic: students ask "what does it MEAN and what does it DO?" without prompting — the **compound lens** effect

### The §2 ↔ §5 Match (Critical Constraint)

This applies identically to both days and is the architecture's load-bearing relationship:

- **A2 DEFINES** the visual rhetoric principle → **A5 CRYSTALLIZES** it in a specific artifact
- **B2 DEFINES** the mathematical property → **B5 CRYSTALLIZES** it in a specific invention
- If you cannot identify what §2 defines and how §5 crystallizes it, the lesson is broken.

### A1: Mythological Introduction

**Cognitive Operation:** PRESENT — "Here is a shape that mattered."

**MAGIC Vector:** [M: 0.1, A: 0.6, G: 0.5, I: 0.9, C: 0.2]
**Loud Actors:** I (LEADS), A (speaking)
**I asks:** "Who said this was sacred? What did it mean?"
**A asks:** "What does this look like in its most symbolically powerful form?"

**Cognitive Affordance:**
> Receive a narrative in which a geometric element enters cultural significance through myth, deity, or cosmological claim — encountering the shape as something that mattered to real people, not as an abstraction.

**What this means:** I(t) selects the GE,C,D node. Ideology pulls a specific geometric element into cultural significance through narrative. Students encounter the GE not as a shape to be studied but as something that carried weight. The emotional and narrative hook establishes significance — the first stage of the GEpHR broadcast. M is quiet because formalization would kill the entry point. G is present but unannounced — the shape IS there, students just don't know they're studying geometry yet.

**Student Demonstration:**
- Can retell the narrative and identify the element's role in it
- Can name what the element symbolized
- Template: "[DEITY/FIGURE] used [ELEMENT] to [NARRATIVE ACTION], establishing [ELEMENT] as symbolizing [CONCEPT]"

**Artifact Relevance:**
- Mythological source texts, deity associations, cosmological narratives
- RWI tags: `i_myth`, `i_deity_association`, `i_cosmological_role`, `a_symbolic_image`, `g_element_identification`
- GEA/GEM: Named but NOT decomposed (A2 does that). A1 presents the shape as a whole.

**E-Textbook Role (Level 5):** Full mythological narrative, image with museum attribution, historical context, discussion prompt, vocabulary.

**Teacher Deliverable (Levels 1+4):**
- Level 1: Element: [name]. Deity: [name]. Hook: [one sentence]. Image: [source].
- Level 4: Narrative with timing cues, image, anticipated student responses, hook delivery guidance.

### A2: Visual Rhetoric — The GEpHR Mechanism

**Cognitive Operation:** DEFINE — "Here is WHY the geometry carries meaning."

**MAGIC Vector:** [M: 0.3, A: 0.8, G: 0.8, I: 0.5, C: 0.2]
**Loud Actors:** A (LEADS), G (CO-LEADS)
**A asks:** "What does this look like? How was it made? What pattern emerges?"
**G asks:** "How is space organized here? What spatial properties produce the perceptual effect?"

**Cognitive Affordance:**
> Decompose the GEA/GEM composition of the element and identify the perceptual affordance that makes the metaphor work — through three stages: (a) Significance (what captures attention), (b) Meaning (what the brain does with those signals), (c) Elaboration (how far the metaphor can travel).

**What this means:** A2 is the conceptual engine of Day A. It takes A1's shape and asks: what about this specific geometric configuration makes it an effective carrier of meaning? This is visual rhetoric analysis — NOT feelings, NOT SEL. It is art theory operating on geometric substrate. The three-stage broadcast (Significance → Meaning → Elaboration) teaches students that geometric elements broadcast across cognition through perceptual grab, interpretive load, and expressive range.

**The Three Sub-stages:**
- **A2a — Significance:** GEA/GEM decomposition happens here. What about this composition captures attention? Compositional analysis: line direction, proportion, symmetry type, contrast, scale.
- **A2b — Meaning:** What does the brain do with those perceptual signals? Geometric property → cognitive interpretation. D₈ symmetry reads as completeness. Radial emanation reads as power/divinity.
- **A2c — Elaboration:** How far can this geometric metaphor travel? Bridges toward A3 by establishing why the element appears across so many contexts.

**Structural Constraints:**
- Whatever visual rhetoric principle is established here MUST reappear in A5 as materially present in a specific artifact
- The A2 principle MUST be the basis of the technique practiced in A6
- The A2 principle and B2's mathematical property are the SAME geometric fact read in two registers

**Student Demonstration:**
- Can name the GEA/GEM elements composing the form
- Can articulate why the geometric property produces a specific perceptual effect
- Can describe the elaboration range (where else could this rhetoric travel?)
- Template: "[ELEMENT] is composed of [GEA COMPONENTS] forming [GEM COMPOSITION] which produces [PERCEPTUAL AFFORDANCE] because [GEOMETRIC PROPERTY], and [CIVILIZATION] exploited this as [COMMUNICATIVE TECHNIQUE]"

**Artifact Relevance:**
- Artifact images with visible GEA/GEM composition, annotated diagrams
- RWI tags: `a_visual_rhetoric`, `g_gea_decomposition`, `g_gem_composition`, `a_perceptual_affordance`, `g_carrier_dimension`, `a_cognitive_effect`
- Layer 1a: Element fully specified; Layer 1b: Construction forensics

**E-Textbook Role (Level 5):** Full three-stage analysis, all diagram types (property isolation, perceptual affordance mapping, cultural application), GEA/GEM notation, carrier comparison, cognitive channel explanation, discussion prompts.

**Teacher Deliverable (Levels 1+4):**
- Level 1: Element: [name]. GEA composition: [list]. Visual rhetoric principle: [one sentence]. Perceptual affordance: [property] → [effect].
- Level 4: Three-stage walkthrough with timing, key diagrams, grade-calibrated language, anticipated questions, differentiation.

### A3: Iconographic Chain — Sacred Art Generalization

**Cognitive Operation:** GENERALIZE (Sacred Domain) — "The visual rhetoric principle appears across sacred imagery."

**MAGIC Vector:** [M: 0.1, A: 0.9, G: 0.7, I: 0.9, C: 0.3]
**Loud Actors:** A (LEADS), I (CO-LEADS), G (speaking)
**A asks:** "What does this look like across different sacred carriers?"
**I asks:** "What meanings persist across these deployments?"

**Cognitive Affordance:**
> Identify the same visual rhetoric mechanism (from A2) operating across multiple carriers within the sacred/mythological domain — recognizing that the same GEA/GEM composition, on different carriers, produces consistent perceptual affordance and consistent meaning.

**What this means:** A3 stays in the I-register for its first generalization. Students see the A2 principle on cylinder seals, temple reliefs, votive sculptures, sacred vessels — different carriers, same geometric rhetoric. The affordance is pattern recognition within a domain: from "this one artifact" to "this is a visual language with consistent grammar." A and I are both high because the sacred context is where the rhetoric was most deliberately deployed.

**Student Demonstration:**
- Can identify the A2 visual rhetoric principle across 3+ sacred carriers
- Can articulate what stays the same (geometric property) vs. what changes (carrier, scale, rendering)
- Template: "[A2 PRINCIPLE] appears on [CARRIER 1], [CARRIER 2], and [CARRIER 3] because [GEA/GEM COMPOSITION] persists across [CARRIER VARIATION], producing consistent [PERCEPTUAL AFFORDANCE]"

**Artifact Relevance:**
- 3–5 images from different sacred carrier types (seal, relief, vessel, architecture, votive)
- RWI tags: `a_iconographic_chain`, `i_sacred_deployment`, `g_cross_carrier_persistence`, `a_visual_narrative`, `i_deific_representation`

**E-Textbook Role (Level 5):** All images with full museum attribution, GEA/GEM annotations per carrier, historical context per artifact, comparison analysis, discussion prompts.

**Teacher Deliverable (Levels 1+4):**
- Level 1: A2 principle: [name]. Sacred carriers: [list of 3–5 with sources]. Pattern: [one sentence].
- Level 4: Images with guided comparison questions, timing, "what's the same across all of these?" prompt structure.

### A4: Diffusion — Ritual, Object, Role, Class, Time

**Cognitive Operation:** GENERALIZE (Secular/Social) — "The visual rhetoric travels beyond the sacred."

**MAGIC Vector:** [M: 0.2, A: 0.7, G: 0.7, I: 0.8, C: 0.6]
**Loud Actors:** I (LEADS), C (RISING), A (speaking), G (speaking)
**I asks:** "Who said this was sacred? What happened when it crossed boundaries?"
**C asks:** "Who controlled this knowledge? Who was allowed? Who was excluded?"

**Cognitive Affordance:**
> Trace the GEpHR as it diffuses through society — from sacred art to everyday objects, administrative tools, personal ornament — identifying who was allowed to use it, what it meant in different social positions, and how power shaped its deployment.

**What this means:** The generalization expands beyond sacred domain (A3) to all of society. This is where I and C become visible as active forces. The same geometric rhetoric appears on pottery, textiles, administrative seals, personal ornament — but NOT equally. A4 has sub-sections (A4a–f) covering sacred/ceremonial, ritual context, official/administrative, domestic/everyday, architectural, personal/wearable. Each sub-section examines a different artifact category and identifies a different signal/meaning.

**Sub-sections:** A4a (Sacred/Ceremonial), A4b (Ritual Context), A4c (Official/Administrative), A4d (Domestic/Everyday), A4e (Architectural), A4f (Personal/Wearable). Minimum 2 sub-sections per lesson, each citing a SPECIFIC named artifact.

**Student Demonstration:**
- Can distinguish elite vs. common carriers of the same element
- Can identify who controlled production, access, or meaning
- Can identify how meaning shifted across social contexts
- Template: "[ELEMENT] on [ELITE CARRIER] was controlled by [POWER HOLDER] because [INSTITUTIONAL REASON], while [ELEMENT] on [COMMON CARRIER] was accessible to [POPULATION] with [DIFFERENT MEANING/FUNCTION]"

**Artifact Relevance:**
- Artifacts stratified by social context, evidence of production control, guild structures, ritual restrictions
- RWI tags: `i_institutionalization`, `c_access_control`, `c_class_stratification`, `a_cross_carrier`, `i_ritual_regulation`, `g_carrier_diversity`

**E-Textbook Role (Level 5):** All sub-sections with full artifact detail, power analysis, access control discussion, cross-class comparison, discussion prompts per sub.

**Teacher Deliverable (Levels 1+4):**
- Level 1: Diffusion path: [sacred] → [secular]. Power constraint: [one sentence]. Key artifacts: [2–3 with sources].
- Level 4: 2–3 selected sub-sections with guided questions about "who got to use this?", differentiation notes.

### A5: Material Culture — Single Artifact Crystallization

**Cognitive Operation:** CRYSTALLIZE (Metaphor) — "Here is ONE artifact where the visual rhetoric principle is materially present."

**MAGIC Vector:** [M: 0.3, A: 0.9, G: 0.8, I: 0.3, C: 0.1]
**Loud Actors:** A (LEADS), G (CO-LEADS)
**A asks:** "How was it made? What technique produced this?"
**G asks:** "What spatial operations does the construction require?"

**Cognitive Affordance:**
> Examine one specific artifact where the A2 visual rhetoric principle is physically present — decomposing the construction technique as a GEA → GEM sequence, learning the craft intelligence that makes the geometric configuration materially real.

**What this means:** A5 is the ANCHOR section. One specific artifact, one specific moment in history. The shift is from reading rhetoric (A2–A4) to understanding how it was made. The A2 GEA/GEM decomposition becomes a construction sequence: first this atomic element, then this one, then their interaction produces the molecular whole. I and C step back. This is about craft — the material intelligence of making.

**Structural Constraint:** Must instantiate the concept from A2. If a student holds the A2 diagram next to the A5 artifact, the match must be visible.

**Student Demonstration:**
- Can name the artifact, its carrier, and its construction technique
- Can trace the GEA → GEM construction sequence in the physical object
- Template: "[ARTIFACT] embodies the A2 principle because [CONSTRUCTION TECHNIQUE] produces [GEA → GEM SEQUENCE], making [VISUAL RHETORIC PRINCIPLE] physically present in [CARRIER MATERIAL]"

**G asks:** "What spatial constraints do you discover by making?"

**What this means:** Students make the thing. Hands on materials. The affordance is embodied discovery — "practice comes before words." Students follow the same compositional logic the ancient makers used (GEA → GEM), experiencing how the molecular composition emerges from atomic steps. C is at minimum: everyone makes, everyone has access.

### A7: Dual-Register Reveal — The Pivot

**Cognitive Operation:** REVEAL + PIVOT — "This shape carries BOTH meaning and function."

**MAGIC Vector:** [M: 0.5, A: 0.7, G: 0.9, I: 0.7, C: 0.6]
**Loud Actors:** G (PEAKS — Day A maximum), all others speaking
**G asks:** "How is space organized here? — and now notice: it MEANS and it DOES."
**M asks:** "Wait — this geometry has PROPERTIES. What if those properties do WORK?"

**Cognitive Affordance:**
> Encounter an architectural or large-scale artifact where the week's geometric element simultaneously does rhetorical work (carries meaning) AND functional work (holds weight, spans space, distributes force) — recognizing that the circumnutating relationship is a structural fact about geometry, not a coincidence.

**What this means:** A7 is the PIVOT — the fulcrum of the entire week. G peaks because the accumulated spatial understanding from A1–A6 reaches the point where dual reading is possible. Students see a building that is BOTH sacred and structural. M rises suddenly. C rises — "Someone FUNDED this." The visual rhetoric doesn't "become" function — it was always ALSO functional.

**Structural Constraint:** Architecture (or equivalent dual-register artifact) is the required carrier. Same geometric element as all week. Same GEA/GEM decomposition from A2, now annotated for BOTH registers.

**Student Demonstration:**
- Can identify both the metaphor reading AND the function reading of the same artifact
- Can articulate that the same geometric property serves both
- Template: "[ARCHITECTURAL ARTIFACT] uses [ELEMENT] to both [METAPHOR: symbolize X via PERCEPTUAL AFFORDANCE] and [FUNCTION: enable Y via MECHANICAL PROPERTY] because [SAME GEA/GEM COMPOSITION] operates in both registers"

**Artifact Relevance:**
- ONE architectural example where both registers are simultaneously visible
- RWI tags: `g_dual_register`, `a_architectural_rhetoric`, `m_structural_function`, `g_gecd_dual_reading`, `i_monumental_meaning`, `c_state_commission`

**E-Textbook Role (Level 5):** Full architectural analysis, dual-register annotation, historical commissioning context, structural analysis, "same shape, two readings" discussion.

A is quiet — diagrams serve clarity, not beauty. I and C are nearly silent: math doesn't need institutional permission to be true.

**The critical A2↔B2 connection:** The same geometric property that creates the visual rhetoric (A2) IS the mathematical property (B2). One property, two registers.

**M asks:** "Now TRANSFORM it. Rotate, reflect, extrude, project. What NEW properties emerge?"
**G asks:** "Static form becomes dynamic operation. The element MOVES through space."

**Cognitive Affordance:**
> Apply GEK-T transformations (rotation, reflection, scaling, projection, tessellation, extrusion) to the element and identify properties that are preserved vs. properties that emerge — moving from static geometry to dynamic spatial operation.

**What this means:** B3 takes B2's formal property and shows it in motion. If B2 said "a circle has equidistance from center," B3 shows what happens when you rotate (sphere), extrude (cylinder), tessellate (it can't — why not?). This is generalization through mathematical operation, parallel to A3's generalization through iconographic variation. G peaks because spatial organization is being actively reconfigured.

**Student Demonstration:**

- Can identify what properties are preserved and what new properties emerge

**G asks:** "How did this spatial capability evolve over time? What combinations emerged?"
**C asks:** "Who funded the engineering? Which innovations were suppressed, monopolized, or lost?"

**Cognitive Affordance:**
> Trace the GEK's functional deployment across time — how the geometric element's mathematical property combined with other elements and dimensional operations to create increasingly complex mechanisms, and how power structures shaped what got built, funded, suppressed, or lost.

**What this means:** B4 is the functional parallel to A4. Where A4 traced the GEpHR's diffusion across social strata, B4 traces the GEK's diffusion across time. This is G(t) — the autoregressive historical trajectory. The arch principle appears in drainage channels, then bridges, then aqueducts, then domes. Each step involves the base property INTERACTING with new elements and new GEK-T operations. C re-enters because mechanical capability is always captured by power structures. The **deviation vector** framework lives here — what COULD have been combined but wasn't, and why.

- Evidence of innovation suppression, monopoly, patronage, lost knowledge
