# Deity Visual Canon — Mesopotamia Pilot

Character consistency across ALL lessons. Each deity has ONE canonical visual description used for AI recreation prompts.

---

## Shamash (Sun God) — Week 1

```yaml
deity: Shamash
akkadian: Šamaš
sumerian: Utu
domain: Sun, Justice, Truth
primary_gea: GEA.circle (sun disk)

visual_canon:
  figure: Bearded male figure seated on a throne
  headdress: Horned crown with four tiers of horns
  garment: Long layered robe (kaunakes) with fringe, covering left shoulder
  right_hand: Holds a rod-and-ring symbol (symbols of divine authority)
  left_hand: Extended, palm open, rays of light emanating from shoulders
  sun_disk: Large rayed disk behind or above head, with wavy rays extending outward
  throne: Sits on a mountain or ziggurat-like platform
  attendants: Two figures with ropes pulling open doors of heaven (dawn scene)
  skin_tone: Warm bronze
  expression: Stern, judicial
  lighting: Golden backlight from sun disk, dramatic shadows

canonical_artifacts:
  - "Shamash Tablet (BM 91000) — seated Shamash with sun disk"
  - "Cylinder seal Walters 42.638 — sun god with rays"
  - "Stele of Hammurabi (Louvre Sb 8) — Shamash giving law"

recreation_prompt: >
  Ultra-realistic museum photograph style: Bearded male deity seated on a
  stepped throne resembling a ziggurat. He wears a four-tiered horned crown
  and a long fringed kaunakes robe. His right hand holds a rod-and-ring
  symbol. A large rayed sun disk radiates golden light from behind his head
  with wavy and straight rays alternating. Two smaller attendant figures
  pull open ornate doors on either side. The scene is rendered as if carved
  in relief on a golden-brown stone tablet, with dramatic side lighting
  emphasizing the carved depth. Style: Archaeological reconstruction,
  Mesopotamian Neo-Babylonian period, circa 860 BCE.
```

## Sin (Moon God) — Week 2

```yaml
deity: Sin
akkadian: Sîn
sumerian: Nanna
domain: Moon, Time, Calendar
primary_gea: GEA.arc (crescent)

visual_canon:
  figure: Bearded male figure, older appearance (father of Shamash)
  headdress: Crescent moon crown — horizontal crescent atop head
  garment: Starred robe (stars = nighttime domain), deep blue/silver
  right_hand: Raised in blessing gesture
  left_hand: Holds a crescent standard (pole with crescent on top)
  crescent: Horizontal crescent (horns pointing upward) — NOT western thin crescent
  mount: Rides a winged bull (his sacred animal)
  attendants: Stars surrounding him (Pleiades cluster)
  skin_tone: Pale silver-blue
  expression: Contemplative, wise
  lighting: Cool moonlight, silvery glow

canonical_artifacts:
  - "Ur-Nammu Stele — Sin at top with crescent"
  - "Cylinder seal with crescent standard"
  - "Kudurru boundary stones — crescent symbol"

recreation_prompt: >
  Ultra-realistic museum photograph style: Elderly bearded male deity
  standing on a winged bull. He wears a starred deep-blue robe and a
  crown topped by a horizontal crescent moon with upward-pointing horns.
  His right hand is raised in blessing, his left holds a tall standard
  topped with a crescent. Small star clusters surround him against a
  dark night sky. The scene is rendered as if carved on a blue-grey
  limestone stele with silver leaf accents on the crescent. Style:
  Archaeological reconstruction, Sumerian Ur III period, circa 2100 BCE.
```

## Ishtar (Star Goddess) — Week 3

```yaml
deity: Ishtar
akkadian: Ištar
sumerian: Inanna
domain: War, Love, Venus (morning/evening star)
primary_gem: GEM.8star (eight-pointed star)

visual_canon:
  figure: Female figure, fierce and beautiful simultaneously
  headdress: Multi-tiered horned crown with eight-pointed star above
  garment: Upper body warrior attire (scale armor), lower body pleated skirt
  right_hand: Holds a scimitar (war aspect)
  left_hand: Holds a double lion leash (two lions at her feet)
  star: Eight-pointed star radiates from behind her — CRITICAL: must be 8 points
  wings: Large feathered wings spread behind her
  lions: Two lions crouching at her feet, facing outward
  skin_tone: Warm golden
  expression: Fierce, commanding, eyes wide
  lighting: Dual lighting — warm gold (morning star) and cool silver (evening star)

canonical_artifacts:
  - "Queen of the Night relief (BM 2003-0718.1)"
  - "Ishtar Gate lions — Pergamon Museum"
  - "Cylinder seals with 8-pointed star"

recreation_prompt: >
  Ultra-realistic museum photograph style: Powerful female deity with
  spread feathered wings, standing on two crouching lions facing outward.
  She wears scale armor on her upper body and a pleated skirt below. A
  multi-tiered horned crown sits on her head with a radiating eight-pointed
  star behind it. Her right hand holds a curved scimitar, her left grips
  leashes controlling the lions. The eight-pointed star is geometrically
  precise — two overlapping squares rotated 45°. The scene is rendered
  as if carved in a terracotta relief plaque with traces of original
  paint in red, blue, and gold. Style: Archaeological reconstruction,
  Old Babylonian period, circa 1800 BCE.
```

## Marduk (King of Gods) — Week 5

```yaml
deity: Marduk
akkadian: Marduk
sumerian: Asar-lu-hi (aspect)
domain: Chief deity of Babylon, storms, creation
primary_gea: GEA.line → spade/marru symbol
secondary_gem: GEM.6star (associated with Marduk's star)

visual_canon:
  figure: Tall powerful male figure, younger and more martial than other gods
  headdress: Elaborate horned crown with many tiers (supreme divine rank)
  garment: Ornate robe with dragon-scale pattern (mushhushshu dragon)
  right_hand: Holds a triangular spade (marru) — the spade of Marduk
  left_hand: Holds a ring/leads mushhushshu dragon by leash
  dragon: Mushhushshu (snake-dragon) at his feet — serpentine neck, horned head, eagle talons, lion forelimbs
  symbol: Triangular spade on a pedestal beside him
  skin_tone: Deep bronze
  expression: Regal, commanding
  lighting: Storm-lit, dramatic cloud backdrop

canonical_artifacts:
  - "Marduk and mushhushshu dragon on Ishtar Gate glazed bricks"
  - "Cylinder seals showing spade symbol"
  - "Babylonian kudurru with Marduk's spade and mushhushshu"

recreation_prompt: >
  Ultra-realistic museum photograph style: Powerful male deity in an
  ornate multi-tiered horned crown and dragon-scale patterned robe.
  He holds a triangular spade (marru) in his right hand. At his feet
  crouches a mushhushshu dragon — a serpentine creature with a horned
  head, eagle talons on its hind legs, and lion forelimbs. A dramatic
  storm sky forms the backdrop. The scene is rendered as if a glazed
  brick panel from the Ishtar Gate, in deep blue, gold, and white
  enamel. Style: Archaeological reconstruction, Neo-Babylonian period,
  circa 600 BCE.
```

```yaml
  garment: Short warrior tunic, belt with thunderbolts

  left_hand: Holds reins of a bull (his sacred animal)
  mount: Standing on or beside a bull
  lightning: Multiple zigzag lines radiating from his hand/clouds above
  rain: Vertical lines descending beneath him (rain)
  skin_tone: Dark bronze
  expression: Fierce, shouting
  lighting: Flash-lit, high contrast

recreation_prompt: >
  Ultra-realistic museum photograph style: Muscular storm deity in

  horned crown wreathed in storm clouds. His right hand thrusts

  Rain lines descend beneath the bull. The scene is rendered as if
  carved on a dark basalt boundary stone with weathered surfaces.
  Style: Archaeological reconstruction, Kassite period, circa 1300 BCE.
```

## Nabu (God of Writing) — Week 6

```yaml
deity: Nabu
akkadian: Nabû
sumerian: Muštešir-ḫabli
domain: Writing, wisdom, scribes
primary_gea: GEA.line (stylus / wedge)

visual_canon:
  figure: Youthful male figure, scholarly bearing
  headdress: Round cap with single horn (lower divine rank, son of Marduk)
  garment: Simple clean robe (scholarly, not martial)
  right_hand: Holds a writing stylus — triangular reed tip
  left_hand: Holds a clay tablet
  symbol: Stylus on a tablet stand beside him
  mount: Mushhushshu dragon (inherited from father Marduk)
  skin_tone: Medium bronze
  expression: Calm, contemplative, focused
  lighting: Warm lamp-like light (interior, scribal school setting)

recreation_prompt: >
  Ultra-realistic museum photograph style: Youthful scholarly deity
  in a clean simple robe and a round cap with a single horn. He sits
  cross-legged holding a triangular-tipped reed stylus in his right
  hand, pressing it into a clay tablet held in his left. A stand
  beside him holds additional tablets. The scene is rendered as if
  a terracotta figurine photographed in museum lighting. Style:
  Archaeological reconstruction, Neo-Assyrian period, circa 700 BCE.
```

```yaml
  headdress: Horned crown with omega (Ω) symbol — her distinctive marker
  garment: Multi-layered pleated robe, earth tones
  right_hand: Extended in blessing over small figures (creation)
  left_hand: Holds a plant or tree branch
  omega: The omega symbol (Ω) prominently displayed — curved arch with downturned ends
  attendants: Small human figures emerging from clay (creation myth)
  skin_tone: Earth brown
  expression: Maternal, powerful, calm
  lighting: Warm earth tones, diffuse natural light

recreation_prompt: >
  Ultra-realistic museum photograph style: Matronly goddess in
  multi-layered earth-toned robes and a horned crown bearing the
  omega (Ω) symbol. She extends her right hand in blessing over
  small human figures. Her left hand holds a flowering tree branch.
  The omega symbol is prominent — a curved arch with downturned
  ends. The scene is rendered as if carved on a limestone votive
  plaque. Style: Archaeological reconstruction, Early Dynastic
  period, circa 2500 BCE.
```

## Usage Rules

1. **Consistency**: ALWAYS use the exact same visual description for a deity across all grade levels and all weeks
2. **Variation**: Different POSES are acceptable — the core elements (headdress, symbols, attributes) must remain identical
3. **Grade Adaptation**: Grade 3 gets simpler compositions, Grade 5 gets more complex backgrounds, but the deity looks THE SAME
4. **Style Suffix**: Always end recreation prompts with "Style: Archaeological reconstruction, [Period], circa [Date]"
5. **No Modern Elements**: Never include modern clothing, technology, or anachronistic details
